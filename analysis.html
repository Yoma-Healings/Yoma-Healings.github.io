BQ 01
Is the layoff crisis getting worse or better year over year?
▲ 2025 is the worst year on record — 200,445 layoffs and still accelerating
Annual Trend · 2020–2025
Total Layoffs by Year

2025 surpassed 2023's Big Tech correction to become the single worst year on record. After a brief recovery signal in 2021, the crisis escalated every year from 2022. The data shows this is a structural shift — not a cycle — driven by AI automation replacing roles across all seniority levels.

BQ 02
Which months trigger the biggest layoff waves? When should companies and workers prepare?
▲ Three major waves identified — Q1 and Q4 are the highest-risk periods every year
Monthly Time Series · Mar 2020 – Dec 2025
Full Monthly Layoff Timeline

Three distinct waves are visible: Apr 2020 (COVID lockdowns hit physical industries), Jan 2023 (Big Tech post-overhiring correction — 66K in one month), and Nov 2025 (AI-driven restructuring — 40K+ monthly). Companies should model workforce risk budgets around Q1 and Q4 exposure windows.

BQ 03
Which industries face the highest risk in the current 2024–2025 economy?
▲ Hardware, Software Development and E-commerce are the new danger zones
Industry Risk · 2024–2025
Top 10 Most Affected Industries
BQ 04 · Geographic Concentration
Top 10 Countries by Total Layoffs

AI automation is eliminating roles in Hardware and Software Development — sectors that previously drove job growth. E-commerce's 2025 surge reflects Amazon and Alibaba restructuring their logistics and tech layers simultaneously.

The USA accounts for 72% of all tracked layoffs — a geographic concentration that exposes the risk of over-dependence on US tech employment. India (7%) and Germany (4%) follow, largely from outsourcing arm reductions.

BQ 05
How has industry vulnerability shifted from the COVID era (2020) to the AI era (2025)?
▲ Travel & Transportation (COVID) replaced by E-commerce & Software Dev (AI) — a fundamental structural shift
COVID Era · 2020
Top Industries — 2020
AI Era · 2025
Top Industries — 2025

In 2020, physical-world industries (Transportation, Travel, Retail) dominated — lockdown effects. By 2025, the pattern completely inverted: digital industries are now the most vulnerable. This transition confirms that AI disruption is not sector-specific — it is economy-wide. Finance leaders should reassess "digital transformation" as a workforce risk, not just an opportunity.

BQ 07 · Severity · Avg % Workforce Cut
Average Layoff % by Funding Stage

Two distinct risk profiles emerge: Post-IPO companies cut the most people in absolute terms — driven by public market shareholder pressure to reduce operating costs immediately after listing. Seed and Series A companies cut the highest percentage — these are often complete shutdowns. Investors should model both dimensions separately when assessing portfolio workforce risk.

BQ 08

▲ Amazon + Intel + Microsoft = 148K workers — 20% of ALL tracked global layoffs

BQ 09 · Chronic Restructuring · Repeat Rounds
Companies with Most Layoff Events

Amazon alone cut 72,244 workers — more than the entire tech workforce of Sweden. The concentration in 3 companies (Amazon, Intel, Microsoft) represents a systemic dependency risk for cities like Seattle and San Francisco where these employers dominate the local economy.

Google and Amazon each conducted 16 separate layoff rounds from 2020–2025. This pattern signals chronic structural instability — not one-time corrections. Companies with 8+ rounds should be flagged by institutional investors as high workforce governance risk.

BQ 12
What is the true economic cost of tech layoffs by industry? Where is capital destruction highest?
▲ $59.7 Billion estimated total — Consumer, Retail and Hardware each destroyed $4B+ in human capital
Economic Impact · $80K Average Tech Salary per Employee
Estimated Capital Cost of Layoffs by Industry (USD Millions)

At an estimated average compensation of $80,000 per tech employee, the total human capital destroyed between 2020–2025 is approximately $59.7 billion — equivalent to the annual GDP of Latvia. Consumer and Retail industries top the list, followed closely by Hardware. This metric should be a core input for any CFO modelling restructuring ROI against the true cost of lost institutional knowledge and rehiring cycles.

Analyst Conclusions

Strategic Takeaways

🔴 Critical Risk
2025 Is an Inflection Point
The 200K+ layoffs in 2025 signal AI is no longer a future threat — it is actively eliminating roles now. Hardware, E-commerce and Software Development companies should expect continued workforce reductions through 2026.
🟡 Investor Signal
Post-IPO Companies Are Highest Risk
375K Post-IPO layoffs confirm public market pressure forces deeper cuts. Institutional investors should price workforce restructuring risk into IPO valuations and monitor companies with 8+ layoff rounds as governance red flags.
🟢 Recovery Signal
Finance & Consumer Show Partial Recovery
Finance and Consumer sector layoffs declined from 2023 peaks, suggesting these industries have completed their major restructuring cycles. Healthcare and Real Estate remain relatively stable — potential safe harbour sectors for employment.
